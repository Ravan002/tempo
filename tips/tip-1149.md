# TIP-1149: Logical Zones with Shared Settlement

## Abstract

An operator that runs several zones today gives each one its own portal, so
anyone can see each zone's escrow balance and which deposits and withdrawals
belong to it. This TIP lets such zones share one portal, deposit queue, and
withdrawal queue, and settle together in one batch. Observers see the group's
combined flows but cannot tell which zone a deposit, withdrawal, or balance
belongs to. Each zone keeps its own execution, accounts, and policies, and zones
in a group can transfer tokens to each other without any public transaction.

## Motivation

A zone gives an enterprise a private execution environment, but its portal on
Tempo is public. With one portal per zone, the portal's escrow is that zone's
balance, and every deposit into and withdrawal from the portal visibly belongs
to that zone. Encrypting recipients does not help: the portal itself identifies
the enterprise.

When one operator runs zones for several enterprises, those zones can hide among
each other by sharing a portal. Sharing the portal address alone is not enough:
per-zone withdrawal roots, deposit cursors, or callback arguments on Tempo would
reveal the association again. The group therefore settles with one public
transition, and everything per-zone stays inside a private proof.

Sharing is limited to zones run by one operator, which already share a trust
boundary. Pooling unrelated operators would let one proof-system failure affect
funds across operators; this TIP does not support it.

## Assumptions

- All members share the operator, sequencer set and threshold, proving
  configuration, deposit encryption keys, and finalized Tempo checkpoint. The
  operator sees every member's data; this TIP provides privacy against public
  observers, not against the operator.
- The verifier authenticates the complete group transition. A sequencer quorum
  alone MUST NOT authorize state or withdrawals, and a verifier that accepts
  unconditionally is insufficient to enable this feature.
- The proof backend keeps member state and execution private. Public proof
  inputs MUST be limited to group-level commitments and public queue data;
  per-zone execution traces or proof outputs MUST NOT be published.
- Replication and recovery include all private group data. Losing decryption
  keys or private state can halt settlement; this TIP adds no data-availability
  guarantee or permissionless escape hatch.
- Shared custody deliberately couples members' safety and liveness: a group-wide
  proof failure or pause affects every member.

# Specification

## Overview

A **logical zone** is an ordinary [TIP-1091](./tip-1091.md) zone, with its own
`zoneId` and the chain ID derived from it, whose factory record points to an
existing portal instead of a portal of its own. A **group** is the set of zones
sharing one portal, called **members** below; the portal address identifies the
group. The zone that created the portal is the group's **primary zone**.

| Public | Private |
| --- | --- |
| Zone IDs, the membership list, and each member's factory record | Which member a deposit, withdrawal, or return belongs to |
| The group's escrow, queues, and settlements | Each member's state, balances, and policies |
| Deposit sender, token, amount, and refund recipient; withdrawal token, amount, recipient, and callback | Deposit destination zone and recipient; withdrawal source zone and sender |

In outline:

- Deposits carry the destination zone ID inside the encrypted payload. Every
  member scans the shared queue and credits only deposits addressed to it
  (section 4).
- Withdrawals from all members go into one queue, under opaque return references
  and in an order that does not reveal members (section 5).
- Members can transfer tokens to each other within a batch without any public
  trace (section 6).
- Each batch settles every member at once and replaces one hiding commitment to
  the group's private state (section 3).

## 1. Group state

Group portals use a new, explicitly versioned settlement and withdrawal ABI and
MUST NOT accept legacy single-zone settlements or withdrawals. Legacy portals
are unchanged.

Public group state, held by the portal:

- the operator configuration and its monotonically increasing version;
- the group proof verifier and configuration hash;
- the group encryption-key history;
- enabled tokens, common portal policies, fees, and pause state;
- the membership list, its hash chain, and pending member approvals;
- the shared deposit queue, processed cursor, and withdrawal queue;
- a `uint64 batchNumber` and one `bytes32 groupStateCommitment`.

Private group state contains each member's canonical block hash, state root, and
deposit cursor; each member's state, including its balances, transaction nonces,
access rules, transfer policies, and return registry; the return-reference key
(section 5); and a commitment salt. It MUST cover every member in the membership
list, including members with no activity.

`groupStateCommitment` MUST be binding and hiding; in particular, it MUST NOT be
an unsalted hash of enumerable member data. Its opening is private input to the
verifier, and the salt MUST stay private to the operator and be refreshed every
batch.

No public event, return value, storage slot, proof input, or settlement
certificate may contain a member's state root, block height, balance, deposit
cursor, or withdrawal root, or attribute a deposit, withdrawal, or return to a
member.

## 2. Creating a group and adding zones

The primary zone is created through the existing factory path with group mode
selected, which installs the portal at that zone's TIP-1091 address as today.
Group mode is fixed at creation and publicly visible. Existing portals are not
converted.

Each further member joins without a new portal:

1. The portal admin, typically the group's management multisig, calls
   `approveLogicalZone(approvalHash)` on the portal, which records the approval
   and emits `LogicalZoneApproved`:

   ```text
   approvalHash = keccak256(abi.encode(APPROVAL_DOMAIN, chainId, portal, admin,
                                       operatorConfigVersion, rpcUrl,
                                       genesisCommitment))
   ```

2. An authorized factory caller calls
   `createLogicalZone(portal, rpcUrl, genesisCommitment)`. The factory
   recomputes the hash with the portal's current admin and operator
   configuration version and consumes the matching approval. It then assigns the
   next zone ID, records the zone with `portal` set to the group portal, appends
   the zone ID to the portal's membership hash chain, and emits `ZoneCreated`.
   It does not install a portal.

The admin approves with a transaction rather than a signature because the
factory precompile cannot make external EVM calls, so it cannot check an
EIP-1271 signature, but it can read portal storage. This works for multisig,
contract, and EOA admins alike. An approval is consumed once and stops matching
if the admin or operator configuration changes. The existing factory caller
restriction still applies, so during the permissioned launch both the factory
owner and the portal admin must act.

`genesisCommitment` is a hiding commitment to the member's genesis state,
including its initial policies; the first batch that includes the member checks
that its initial state opens it. The member's chain ID is derived from its zone
ID as for any zone. Its factory record copies the portal's admin, sequencers,
threshold, verifier, and access modes at creation, but the portal's current
configuration is authoritative.

A new member starts with zero supply at the group's current deposit cursor, so
it cannot claim deposits processed before it joined. A batch applies every
membership addition up to its Tempo checkpoint before processing its deposits,
so a member's creation and its first deposits MAY settle in the same batch.

Operator-set replacement and key rotation apply to the whole group and
invalidate certificates under the previous configuration. Removing members,
moving them between groups, and per-member operator or proof configurations are
out of scope.

## 3. Shared batches

Each batch contains a nonempty sequence of blocks for every member; members
without user transactions produce system-only blocks. All members end at the
same processed deposit number and Tempo checkpoint, so no member can be omitted
to bypass deposit handling, private policies, or accounting checks.

The verifier MUST establish that:

1. the previous private state opens the previous commitment;
2. every member in the membership list at the batch's checkpoint makes a valid
   chain transition, including signature, nonce, token, and access-policy rules,
   from its committed head, or from its genesis commitment if it joins in this
   batch;
3. every member processes the same new deposit-queue prefix in order;
4. each deposit and return has exactly one outcome across the group;
5. the combined withdrawal queue contains exactly the withdrawals and deposit
   refunds these transitions produce, each once;
6. internal transfers conserve value and complete within the batch;
7. the next commitment covers the complete next private state.

Settlement atomically replaces the commitment and appends one combined
withdrawal-queue root. Its public inputs bind the Tempo chain ID, portal, ABI
version, operator configuration version, batch number, previous and next
commitments, finalized Tempo checkpoint and anchor, deposit-prefix hashes and
cursors, token-enablement and membership progress, combined withdrawal root, and
verifier configuration hash. Threshold signatures MUST bind the same statement
under a separate group-settlement domain. Replays, stale configurations,
discontinuous cursors, and substitution of any field MUST fail without changing
portal state.

Existing anchor validation, queue-capacity bounds, and sequencing rules apply at
group scope. A token enablement is acknowledged once every member has processed
it. Per-member transitions stay private even if the implementation aggregates
several proofs.

## 4. Deposits

Deposits use the shared queue and the group's encryption keys. They keep the
existing ECIES/ECDH and authenticated-encryption construction, under a distinct
group-deposit domain and with this fixed 64-byte plaintext:

```text
version:        uint8       = 1
zoneId:         uint32      (big-endian)
recipient:      address     (20 bytes)
memo:           bytes32
reserved:       bytes7      (all zero)
```

The zone ID and recipient are authenticated ciphertext. Key indices MUST select
group keys, never per-member keys. Domain separation MUST bind the Tempo chain,
portal, format version, sender, token, amount, and Tempo refund recipient, in
addition to the existing key and ephemeral-key inputs. Existing replay
protection for routed deposits MUST be preserved with group-scoped
source-withdrawal identity.

Every member processes every queue entry, decrypting it with the same
operator-supplied decryption data, verified as for a single zone today, so all
members classify each entry identically:

- **Addressed to this member:** the member credits it under its admission and
  token policies or, if they reject it, refunds it through its outbox, as a zone
  does today.
- **Addressed to another member:** no effect.
- **No valid destination** (authentication failure, unknown version, nonzero
  reserved bytes, or a zone ID that is not a member at the batch's checkpoint):
  the primary zone refunds it through its outbox, and every other member skips
  it.

Each failed deposit therefore produces exactly one refund to its Tempo refund
recipient. Refunds from the primary zone and from a rejecting member look the
same on Tempo, and no public receipt or rejection reason reveals which zone a
deposit targeted.

## 5. Withdrawals and returns

### Return references

Public withdrawal fields stay as today (token, amount, recipient, callback gas
and data), except that the zone-local `uint64 fallbackNonce` becomes a
`bytes32 returnRef`. Zero stays reserved: user withdrawals MUST carry a nonzero
`returnRef`, and `returnRef == 0` marks a deposit refund, which the portal
handles through the terminal refund path.

The member that accepts a withdrawal assigns its reference, as the zone outbox
assigns `fallbackNonce` today, from a per-member counter that starts at 1:

```text
returnRef = keccak256(RETURN_REF_DOMAIN || returnRefKey || zoneId || counter)
```

`zoneId` is a big-endian `uint32` and `counter` a big-endian `uint64`. The
counter increment, reference allocation, and debit happen in one transaction,
the verifier recomputes the reference, and the member's receipt returns it so
the user can track the withdrawal on Tempo. References are unique across members
by construction.

`returnRefKey` is a long-lived 32-byte group secret. Without it, anyone could
recompute every reference from the public zone IDs and sequential counters and
attribute each withdrawal to its member. It is supplied to member execution as a
private input and MUST NOT appear in member state, blocks, receipts, or RPC
responses. Replacing it does not affect uniqueness, because counters continue.

Each member keeps a return registry that maps its references to private fallback
recipients, as the zone outbox does today. The registry stores no token or
amount, because returns carry both. Withdrawal fees follow the group fee
schedule, cannot reveal a member-specific fee recipient, and are not refunded on
return.

### Queue order

Each batch's combined queue lists the batch's deposit refunds in the order of
the deposits they refund, followed by its user withdrawals in a keyed random
merge that preserves each member's own order. Ordering by member instead would
put each member's withdrawals in one visible run.

1. Each member orders its withdrawals for the batch by block, transaction, and
   record. For `n` withdrawals it computes
   `keccak256(ORDER_DOMAIN || returnRefKey || batchNumber || zoneId || j)` for
   `j = 1..n`, with `batchNumber` and `j` as big-endian `uint64`, sorts the
   values, and assigns them to its withdrawals in order.
2. The queue lists all user withdrawals by ascending assigned value, breaking
   ties by `returnRef`.

This yields a uniformly random interleaving of the members' sequences. The
assigned values are never published; the verifier recomputes them and checks the
order. The public encoding MUST NOT contain member boundaries or per-member
counts. Withdrawals from one member reach Tempo in that member's order, as for a
single zone today; the order between members within a batch is unspecified.

### Delivery and returns

Delivery keeps today's revert-isolated behavior. A successful delivery needs no
further action; its registry entry remains, as fallback-recipient entries do
today. A failed delivery leaves the funds in escrow, and the portal appends a
return entry `(returnRef, token, refundableAmount)` to the deposit queue, where
`refundableAmount` is the withdrawal's `amount`. The portal derives these fields
from the consumed withdrawal, not from sequencer input, and the entry contains
no zone ID or fallback recipient.

The member whose registry contains the reference credits the token and amount to
the fallback recipient once and deletes the entry; other members skip it. If the
mint is blocked, the credit is parked in that member's private refund registry
and never bounced again. Each reference comes from one member, and its entry is
removed only by its own return, so exactly one member recognizes each return; if
none does, the batch is invalid. Return registries MUST be part of committed,
replicated private state, not a sequencer-local lookup, so a replacement
sequencer can recover pending returns from that state and finalized Tempo
history.

A successful callback cannot both keep the transfer and produce a return, queue
entries are consumed once, and a retry cannot create a second refund. Existing
capacity reservations MUST cover the maximum number of possible returns. Deposit
refunds use the existing terminal Tempo refund registry and MUST NOT create a
return reference or a second bounce-back.

### Callbacks

Sender tags and optional encrypted sender data MUST use group-scoped domains and
MUST NOT encode the source member. The shared messenger authenticates the group
portal and passes the reserved zone ID `0` instead of the source zone ID;
receivers identify the group by `sourcePortal`. Contracts that require a nonzero
source zone ID or a public source member are incompatible, and this TIP adds no
disclosure mode.

## 6. Transfers between members

A member offers a private transfer whose signed request binds the source chain,
group portal, destination zone ID, token, amount, recipient, and the sender's
ordinary transaction nonce. Only members of the same group are valid
destinations. The source enforces the sender's signature, balance, allowance
where applicable, and policies; the destination enforces its admission, token,
and recipient policies.

Each batch settles transfers in two phases:

1. After the shared deposit prefix is processed, members execute ordinary
   blocks. A transfer request locks its amount in the source balance and emits a
   private transfer record. The lock is a system reservation, not a transfer to
   another account; the amount cannot be spent or withdrawn.
2. After all ordinary blocks, source and destination system blocks settle the
   records in ascending source zone ID, block number, transaction index, and
   request index, against the policies in effect at the end of ordinary
   execution. An accepted transfer burns the locked amount at the source and
   mints exactly that amount at the destination. A rejected transfer releases
   the lock, which a policy change cannot prevent. No destination user code
   runs.

All records settle before the batch's commitment is accepted, and credits are
spendable from the next batch. Source and destination effects form one atomic
transition: a transfer touches neither escrow nor the public queues, makes no
public callback, and emits no Tempo event of its own; only the group commitment
changes. A rejected transfer does not affect others, but an invalid proof or a
missing system outcome invalidates the whole batch. Execution fees follow the
member's private rules; no bridge fee is charged.

Transfers never stay pending across batches, and there are no destination
contract calls, cross-group transfers, or general cross-zone messages. If a
batch fails or is reorganized, its locks and credits revert together, and
recovery re-executes the same ordered records.

## 7. Accounting, policies, and privacy

The verifier enforces conservation per token and per member. A member's supply
changes only through an authenticated deposit, its own authorized withdrawal, a
matched internal transfer, or an authenticated return, so sharing custody never
lets one member's activity debit another.

Group escrow also covers deposits not yet credited, withdrawals not yet paid,
returns not yet credited, parked refunds, and fee liabilities, and a transition
MUST count each amount in exactly one of these categories. Paying or refunding
an external obligation removes exactly that liability; internal transfers change
neither total liabilities nor escrow. Token issuers' burn and freeze powers can
still break solvency or liveness, as today.

Portal-level policies are common and public; member policies are checked
privately. A portal-level policy that depends on which member a deposit or
withdrawal belongs to cannot be enforced without breaking the privacy goal. RPC
access stays scoped to each member and account. Group decryption witnesses,
other members' return registries, the return-reference key, and commitment salts
MUST NOT be exposed through member block or trace RPCs.

The guarantee is that nothing public names a member; it is not statistical
unlinkability. Amounts, tokens, counterparties, callback data, timing, operator
disclosures, and low group activity can still identify a member. Internal
transfers MUST NOT require separate public submissions, and adding a member MUST
NOT require a settlement of its own. Operators SHOULD settle on a common
cadence; padding and cover traffic are not required.

## 8. Compatibility and activation

Activation requires a coordinated upgrade of the factory, portal, messenger,
verifier, sequencer, prover, and clients. Legacy zone creation remains
available, existing portals and their deposits, withdrawals, and certificates
continue under their current rules, and no escrow or live queue moves. Migrating
a legacy zone into a group requires a separate proposal.

Shared zones change TIP-1091 in three places:

- A member's portal is not at `ZONE_PORTAL_PREFIX || zoneId`, which holds only
  for the primary zone. Clients, nodes, and indexers MUST read it from
  `zones(zoneId).portal`. `isZonePortal` is unchanged.
- A zone node MUST validate its chain ID against its own zone ID, not one read
  from the portal.
- The shared messenger MUST accept zone ID `0` from a group-mode portal and
  keeps checking that any nonzero zone ID belongs to the calling portal.

Each member's factory record supplies its RPC endpoint and the portal supplies
the encryption keys, so depositing needs only the public zone ID.

## 9. Open items before implementation readiness

This draft is not an activation-ready ABI. Before implementation readiness,
specify and review:

- the group creation, `approveLogicalZone`, `createLogicalZone`, settlement,
  withdrawal, and messenger interfaces, error selectors, and storage layouts;
- the commitment tree, canonical encodings, hash domains (including
  `APPROVAL_DOMAIN`, `RETURN_REF_DOMAIN`, and `ORDER_DOMAIN`), encryption
  transcript, proof public-input encoding, and reference test vectors;
- return-registry growth from successful withdrawals, and any pruning rule,
  which MUST NOT delete an entry whose return may still be queued;
- measured limits for members, blocks, proof work, deposits, withdrawals, and
  transfer records, including worst-case queue scanning;
- forced withdrawals, recovery, and compatibility with public counterparties.
  The shared deposit-queue capacity is to be resolved together with forced
  withdrawals: one member's withdrawals can use up the bounce-back reservation
  and block deposits for every member;
- the implementation owner, reviewed proof backend, and target protocol version.

These are review gates, not permission to drop any requirement above.

# Tooling

Wallets and SDKs MUST look up a zone's portal through its factory record and
support group deposit encryption, return references from member receipts, group
callback encoding, and internal transfers. They MUST report internal transfers
as pending until the batch settles and distinguish rejection from settlement
failure. Public receipt and explorer interfaces MUST show group-level progress
without inventing per-member balances or attribution.

Provisioning MUST add members through a portal-admin approval followed by
factory creation. Sequencers, replicas, and provers MUST use the same
membership, queue cursor, transfer and withdrawal ordering, and commitment
format. Backups MUST capture the member heads, salts, encryption-key history,
return-reference key, and all member states, including return registries, at one
consistent checkpoint. Recovery tooling MUST verify that restored state opens
the accepted commitment before producing blocks.

Conformance fixtures MUST include at least two members with overlapping account
addresses and nonces, encrypted deposits to both, invalid deposits, failed
withdrawals, several withdrawals from each member in one batch, operator and key
rotation, and internal transfers.

# Observability

Group-mode interfaces MUST emit the following public events. Their final
Solidity signatures and indexing are part of the ABI review gate.

| Event | Required fields | Operational question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ZoneCreated (existing) | Zone ID, portal, admin, sequencers, threshold, RPC URL | Which zones exist, and which portal serves each? |
| LogicalZoneApproved | Portal, admin, approval hash | Did the portal admin approve a new member? |
| GroupBatchSubmitted | Portal, batch number, previous/next commitments, Tempo checkpoint, processed deposit number, combined withdrawal queue index/root | Did shared settlement advance continuously? |
| GroupWithdrawalProcessed | Portal, nonzero return reference, token, delivered amount, public recipient | Was a user withdrawal paid? |
| GroupReturnQueued | Portal, return reference, token, refundable amount, deposit number | Which failed withdrawal awaits private credit? |

Existing deposit, terminal-refund, token, key, operator, and pause events remain
group-scoped and MUST NOT gain member identifiers. Deposit refunds emit the
existing terminal-refund events, not GroupWithdrawalProcessed, and internal
transfers emit no public event. Private authenticated telemetry MUST report
per-member progress, failed deposits, pending returns, transfer outcomes, and
accounting discrepancies, and access to it MUST follow member data-access rules.
Public metrics MUST NOT label members or publish their balances or traffic.
Alerts distinguish a member execution error from the group-wide settlement stall
it causes.

# Threat Model

- **Users and depositors:** May submit malformed or replayed deposits,
  double-spend, or target another member. Encryption authentication, signatures,
  nonces, and group conservation must reject these.
- **Operator and sequencers:** Know all private state, control inclusion and
  availability, and can disclose member information. They MUST NOT be able to
  authorize unbacked credits, redirect refunds, or debit users merely by signing
  a batch.
- **Portal admin:** Controls group configuration and approves new members, which
  grants no power over member balances. Existing administrative recovery powers,
  if retained, MUST be documented as an explicit exception to proof-enforced
  safety before activation.
- **Factory caller:** During the permissioned launch only the factory owner can
  create zones, and it cannot add a member to a group without the portal admin's
  approval.
- **Prover and verifier:** Must enforce every member transition and group rule.
  A soundness failure can put all of the group's funds at risk; separate member
  state does not contain that loss, but other groups keep separate escrow.
- **Public observers and callback recipients:** See group transactions, token
  movements, opaque references, and callback data. Nothing public names the
  source zone, but correlation and voluntary disclosure remain possible.
- **Token issuers:** Keep their policy, pause, mint, and burn powers. This TIP
  does not remove them or imply unconditional redeemability.

# Invariants

The implementation and proof-system test suite MUST cover:

1. Every accepted batch opens its predecessor commitment, includes every member,
   and advances one common authenticated deposit prefix without omission.
2. A valid deposit credits only its destination, and other members are
   unaffected. A deposit with no valid destination produces one refund, from the
   primary zone; a deposit its destination rejects produces one refund, from
   that member.
3. Each user withdrawal comes from one member debit and each deposit refund from
   one failed deposit, and each appears once in the combined queue. Nothing
   public labels the issuing member, and queue order follows section 5: refunds
   in deposit order, then the keyed merge, preserving each member's own order.
4. A return reference is derived once, never recurs across members, is
   recognized by exactly one member's return registry, and yields at most one
   credit of the returned token and amount.
5. Successful delivery and a return credit are mutually exclusive, and deposit
   refunds and withdrawal returns terminate without a loop.
6. Per-token accounting counts every outstanding obligation exactly once;
   internal transfers preserve backing and cannot spend escrow twice; equal
   account addresses in different members are distinct accounts.
7. A rejected internal transfer restores the source funds, and an accepted one
   debits and credits atomically. Replays, batch failure, reorganization, and
   recovery cannot leave a one-sided transfer or an unreleased lock.
8. Member creation, operator changes, stale signatures, disabled tokens, policy
   rejection, malformed ciphertext, empty member blocks, queue saturation, and
   failed callbacks preserve these properties.
9. Changing any signed public settlement input invalidates the certificate, and
   changing any private member transition invalidates the proof.
10. Recovery from replicated state produces the same commitment, deposit
    handling, transfer and withdrawal order, and pending returns as
    uninterrupted execution.
11. Public calldata, logs, storage, RPC responses, and proof inputs attribute no
    deposit, withdrawal, or return to a member and reveal no per-member state or
    balances. This is a structural privacy test, not a claim to defeat
    statistical correlation.
12. A zone joins a group only through an unconsumed approval from the portal's
    current admin under the current operator configuration. Settlement binds the
    membership hash chain, and a joining member's initial state opens its
    genesis commitment.

Before this TIP can be scheduled, it requires conformance tests for these cases,
engineering and security review of the group verifier, and performance
measurements at the selected limits.
