# TIP-1149: Logical Zones with Shared Settlement

## Abstract

Allow multiple logical zones run by the same operator to share one portal,
deposit queue, and withdrawal queue. A shared batch verifies blocks from every
member zone while keeping deposit routing, withdrawal origins, and per-zone
balances private. Logical zones retain separate execution and account access.
Token transfers between members settle atomically inside a shared batch without
an individual public transaction or message.

## Motivation

A separate portal for each zone exposes that zone's aggregate balances and the
association between its users' public deposits and withdrawals and the zone.
Encrypting the recipient alone does not hide which enterprise a portal serves.
Enterprises should be able to retain separate execution environments without
publishing their individual escrow balances or identifying every entry and exit.

The intended unit of shared custody is one operator group. Pooling unrelated
operators would expand a proof-system failure's impact across operator trust
boundaries. This TIP does not provide that form of pooling.

Sharing a portal address without changing settlement is insufficient: public
per-zone withdrawal roots, queue cursors, or callback arguments would preserve
the association. The group therefore has one public settlement transition and
keeps its member transitions private.

## Assumptions

- All members use the same operator, sequencer set and threshold, proving
  configuration, deposit encryption keys, and finalized Tempo checkpoint. The
  operator already has access to every member's data. This TIP provides privacy
  against public observers, not against that operator.
- The verifier authenticates the complete group transition. A sequencer quorum
  alone MUST NOT authorize arbitrary state or withdrawals. A prototype verifier
  that accepts unconditionally is insufficient to enable this feature.
- The proof backend keeps member state and execution private. Public proof
  inputs MUST be limited to group-level commitments and public queue data;
  individual execution traces or per-zone proof outputs MUST NOT be published.
- Replication and recovery include all committed private group data. Losing
  decryption keys or private state can halt settlement; this TIP does not solve
  data availability or introduce a permissionless escape hatch.
- Shared custody and settlement deliberately couple member safety and liveness.
  A group-wide proof failure or pause can affect all members. Separate logical
  execution does not restore the isolation of separate portals.

# Specification

## 1. Group identity and state

A **group** is the set of logical zones sharing a portal. Its public identifier
is the portal's existing `zoneId` under [TIP-1091](./tip-1091.md); this identifier
no longer identifies an individual execution environment for group portals.
The portal address, public factory record, and common operator configuration
identify the group. A **logical zone ID** is a private, nonzero `uint32` identifier
unique within that group. Logical zone IDs are never reused.

Group portals use a new, explicitly versioned settlement and withdrawal ABI.
Legacy portals retain their existing ABI and behavior. A group portal MUST NOT
accept legacy single-zone settlements or withdrawals.

Public group state contains:

- the active operator configuration and its monotonically increasing version;
- the group proof verifier and configuration hash;
- the group encryption-key history;
- enabled tokens, common portal policies, fees, and pause state;
- the shared deposit queue, processed cursor, and withdrawal queue;
- a `uint64 batchNumber` and one `bytes32 groupStateCommitment`.

Private group state contains a member registry; each member's chain identity,
canonical block hash, state root, and common deposit cursor; a group return
registry; private administrative nonces; and a commitment salt. Member state
includes its own balances, transaction nonces, access rules, and transfer
policies. The registry MUST authenticate the complete membership, including
members unchanged by ordinary user transactions.

The group commitment MUST be binding and hiding. In particular, it MUST NOT be
an unsalted hash of a publicly enumerable member configuration or balance vector.
Its opening is authenticated private input to the group verifier. Commitment
salts MUST remain private to the operator and be refreshed for each accepted
batch. The exact commitment tree and encoding are an implementation-readiness
item below.

No public group event, return value, storage slot, proof input, or settlement
certificate may contain an individual member's ID, state root, block height,
balance, deposit cursor, withdrawal root, or membership-change payload.

## 2. Creation and administration

The existing factory creation authorization remains in force. An authorized
factory caller MAY create a portal in group mode, initially containing one
logical zone. Group mode is fixed at creation and publicly distinguishable from
legacy mode. This TIP does not silently convert an existing legacy portal.

The group portal admin MAY authorize creation of another member without
creating another portal or public factory entry. The private creation command
binds the Tempo chain ID, group portal, operator configuration version,
administrative nonce, new logical zone ID, chain identity, genesis commitment,
and member policy configuration. The command is signed by the current public
portal admin and verified inside the group transition. Its nonce is consumed
exactly once; an invalid signature, reused ID or chain identity, stale
configuration, or replayed nonce invalidates the transition.

New members start with zero token supply and the group's current processed
deposit cursor. They cannot claim earlier deposits. Membership changes execute
at a batch boundary before routing that batch's new deposits. Creation and its
first deposits MAY settle in the same batch in that order.

Member removal, migration between groups, and independent member operator or
proof configurations are out of scope. Existing operator-set replacement and
key rotation apply to the whole group and invalidate certificates under the
previous configuration. Admin signatures are checked against the admin at the
batch's authenticated Tempo checkpoint, using the existing finality rules.

## 3. Shared batches

A shared batch contains a nonempty ordered sequence of blocks for every member
active during that batch. Members without user transactions execute system-only
blocks to advance their common deposit cursor. All members finish at the same
processed deposit number and authenticated Tempo checkpoint. A member cannot
be omitted to bypass deposit handling, private policies, or accounting checks.

The verifier MUST establish:

1. The previous private state opens the portal's previous group commitment.
2. Every member follows its own valid chain transition, including signature,
   nonce, token, and access-policy rules, from its previously committed head.
3. Every member scans exactly the same newly consumed deposit prefix in order.
4. Each deposit or return has exactly one terminal group-level outcome.
5. The combined withdrawal queue contains exactly the authorized withdrawals
   and deposit refunds produced by these transitions, each once.
6. Internal transfers conserve value and finish within the batch.
7. The next commitment covers the complete next private group state.

Settlement replaces the previous commitment and appends one combined withdrawal
queue root atomically. Public inputs bind the Tempo chain ID, portal, ABI version,
operator configuration version, batch number, previous and next commitments,
finalized Tempo checkpoint and anchor, deposit prefix hashes and cursors,
token-enablement progress, combined withdrawal root, and verifier configuration
hash. Threshold signatures MUST bind this same complete statement using a
separate group-settlement domain. Replays, stale configurations, discontinuous
cursors, and substitution of any field MUST fail without changing portal state.

Existing anchor validation, queue-capacity bounds, and sequencing requirements
continue to apply at group scope. A group-wide token enablement is acknowledged
only when every member has processed it. Per-zone transitions remain private
witnesses even if the implementation internally aggregates multiple proofs.

## 4. Encrypted deposit discrimination

User deposits share one portal queue and one group encryption-key history.
Keep the existing ECIES/ECDH and authenticated-encryption construction, but use
a distinct group-deposit domain and this fixed 64-byte plaintext:

```text
version:        uint8       = 1
logicalZoneId:  uint32      (big-endian)
recipient:      address     (20 bytes)
memo:           bytes32
reserved:       bytes7      (all zero)
```

The recipient and logical zone ID are authenticated ciphertext. Public key
indices MUST select group keys, never keys specific to one member. Domain
separation MUST bind the Tempo chain, portal, format version, public sender,
token, amount, and Tempo refund recipient in addition to the existing key and
ephemeral-key inputs. Existing replay protection for routed deposits MUST be
preserved with group-scoped source-withdrawal identity.

Each member advances its cursor for every entry. A valid deposit for another
member is a no-op for that member, not a rejection or refund. Only the named
member attempts the credit and applies its admission and token policies.

Authentication failure, unknown version, nonzero reserved bytes, an unknown
member, or destination rejection creates exactly one refund obligation to the
public Tempo refund recipient. A designated group routing step handles invalid
or unknown destinations before member execution so no member independently
refunds the same entry. Every queue entry advances despite rejection.

Public sender, token, amount, and Tempo refund recipient remain visible. No
public receipt or rejection reason identifies the selected logical zone.

## 5. Withdrawals and private bounce-back routing

Members produce withdrawals into one combined queue. Queue order is the private
batch's deterministic member/block/transaction/record order; public encoding exposes
only the flattened queue and MUST NOT contain member boundaries or per-member
counts. Public withdrawal fields retain the token, amount, recipient, callback
gas and data, and replace the zone-local `uint64 fallbackNonce` with a nonzero
`bytes32 returnRef`.

The requester supplies a fresh uniformly random 32-byte reference in the signed
private withdrawal request. Before accepting it, the group checks uniqueness
against all previously allocated return references, including retired ones.
The private registry binds it to the source member, token, gross refundable
amount, and private fallback recipient. Allocation and the debit are committed
in the same batch. Fees follow the group fee schedule and cannot expose a
member-specific fee recipient. User-supplied references provide opacity only
when generated with sufficient randomness; first-party clients MUST do so.

For authenticated callbacks, sender tags and optional encrypted sender data
MUST use group-scoped domains and MUST NOT encode the source member. The shared
messenger authenticates the group portal and supplies the public group ID in
place of a logical zone ID. Contracts requiring a public source-member identity
are incompatible with this private callback mode; no implicit disclosure mode
is introduced by this TIP.

A successful delivery retires the pending return obligation when its finalized
Tempo outcome is consumed in a later group batch. A failed delivery leaves funds
in escrow and appends a group return entry containing `(returnRef, token,
refundableAmount)` to the shared deposit queue. The portal derives these fields
from the consumed withdrawal, not from unauthenticated sequencer input. The
entry is public but contains no logical zone ID or fallback recipient.

Every member scans the return entry. The committed registry privately selects
exactly one source member and recipient. The verifier checks the finalized
failure, reference, token, and amount against the pending obligation, credits
that member once, and retires the obligation. If the destination mint is blocked,
park the credit in that member's private refund registry; never bounce it again.
Retired references remain reserved against reuse. Unknown or conflicting return
entries invalidate a batch rather than creating an unbacked credit.

Delivery effects and failure detection retain the existing revert-isolated
withdrawal behavior. Successful callbacks cannot both keep an outgoing transfer
and produce its return. Portal queue entries are consumed once, and existing
capacity reservations MUST cover the maximum number of possible returns.
A retry cannot allocate a second refund. Failed deposit refunds retain the
existing terminal Tempo refund registry and MUST NOT create a user return
reference or a second bounce-back cycle.

Private routing information MUST be part of committed, replicated state, not
only a sequencer-local lookup. Replacement sequencers must recover pending
returns from that state and finalized Tempo history.

## 6. Direct transfers between logical zones

A member exposes a private token-transfer operation whose signed request binds
source chain, group portal, destination logical zone ID, token, amount,
recipient, and the sender's ordinary transaction nonce. Only members of the
same group are valid destinations. The source enforces the sender's ordinary
signature, balance, allowance where applicable, and policy rules. The destination
enforces its admission, token, and recipient policies.

Transfers use a deterministic two-phase batch schedule:

1. After processing the shared deposit prefix, members execute ordinary blocks.
   A direct-transfer request reserves its amount against the source balance
   and emits a private transfer record. Reservation is a system balance lock,
   not a transfer to another account; it cannot be spent or withdrawn.
2. After all ordinary blocks, the group processes records in ascending source
   logical zone ID, source block number, transaction index, and request index
   within the transaction. Source and destination system blocks settle each
   record against the policies in effect after ordinary execution. On acceptance,
   burn the reserved source amount and mint exactly that amount in the
   destination. On rejection, clear the reservation without a token transfer;
   a policy change therefore cannot prevent releasing the lock. No destination
   user code is called.
3. All records finish before the next group commitment is accepted. Credits are
   spendable by ordinary transactions starting in the next shared batch.

Source and destination success are one atomic group transition. No internal
transfer changes portal escrow, appends to either public queue, invokes a public
callback, or causes its own Tempo event. The ordinary group commitment changes.
A rejected request does not invalidate unrelated transfers; an invalid proof or
missing system outcome invalidates the entire batch. Ordinary execution fees
follow the member's private rules; no public bridge fee is charged.

There are no pending transfers across accepted group batches, destination
contract calls, cross-group transfers, or general cross-zone messages. If a
batch fails or is reorganized, all its source reservations and destination
credits revert together. Recovery re-executes the same ordered records.

## 7. Accounting, policies, and privacy

The verifier enforces conservation separately for each token and each member.
Member supply changes only through an authenticated deposit, its own authorized
withdrawal, a matched internal transfer, or an authenticated return. Sharing
custody never permits an unauthorized debit from another member.

Group escrow accounting also includes public deposits not yet credited,
committed withdrawals not yet paid, returns not yet credited, parked refunds,
and fee liabilities. A transition MUST NOT count an amount in two liability
categories. Paying or refunding an external obligation removes exactly its
corresponding liability; an internal transfer changes neither total group
liabilities nor portal escrow. Token-issuer burn or freeze powers retain their
existing trust assumptions and may break solvency or liveness independently.

Portal-level policies remain common and public. Member-specific policies are
checked privately in the group transition. A policy requiring a public logical
zone identifier cannot be enforced without weakening this TIP's privacy goal.
RPC access remains scoped to each member and account; shared proving authority
does not grant ordinary users access to other members' state or queue routing.
Group decryption witnesses, routing-system inputs, return-registry openings, and
commitment salts MUST NOT be exposed through member block or trace RPCs.

The privacy guarantee removes explicit public member attribution. It does not
guarantee statistical unlinkability: amounts, token choice, counterparties,
callback data, timing, operator disclosures, and low group activity can identify
a member. Membership changes or internal transfers MUST NOT force separate
public submissions. Operators SHOULD use a common settlement cadence; padding
and cover traffic are not required by this TIP.

## 8. Compatibility and activation

Activation requires a coordinated protocol upgrade to the factory, portal,
messenger, verifier, sequencer, prover, and clients. Group creation uses an
explicit new mode and group settlement version; legacy creation remains
available. Existing portals and existing deposits, withdrawals, and certificates
continue under their original rules. No existing escrow or live queue is moved
by this TIP. Migrating a legacy zone into a group requires a separate proposal.

The group factory record supplies the group RPC endpoint and common encryption
keys. Member chain identities and routing descriptors are distributed privately
to authorized clients. Clients MUST distinguish the public group ID from the
private logical zone ID and MUST NOT derive the portal address from the latter.

## 9. Items required before implementation readiness

This is a protocol design draft, not an activation-ready ABI. Before advancing
to implementation readiness, specify and review:

- exact factory creation, private administrative command, group settlement,
  withdrawal, and messenger interfaces, error selectors, and storage layouts;
- the hiding commitment tree, canonical encodings, hash domains, encryption
  transcript, proof public-input encoding, and reference test vectors;
- finalized success-receipt authentication and return-registry retirement;
- measured limits for members, blocks, proof work, deposits, withdrawals, and
  internal transfer records, including worst-case queue scanning;
- compatibility with planned forced exits, recovery, and public counterparties;
- the implementation owner, reviewed proof backend, and target protocol version.

These are review gates, not permission to omit the specified routing,
conservation, privacy, or atomicity requirements.

# Tooling

Wallets and SDKs MUST support private member routing descriptors, versioned
group deposit encryption, random return references, group callback encoding,
and internal transfer requests. They MUST report internal transfers as pending
until the whole shared batch settles and distinguish rejection from settlement
failure. Public receipt and explorer interfaces MUST show group-level progress
without inventing per-member balances or attribution.

Provisioning MUST create members through authenticated private admin commands.
Sequencers, replicas, and provers MUST use the same membership, queue cursor,
transfer ordering, and commitment format. Backup and restore MUST include the
member registry, salts, encryption-key history, return registry, and all member
states at a consistent group checkpoint. Recovery tooling MUST verify that the
restored state opens the accepted portal commitment before producing blocks.

Conformance fixtures MUST include at least two members with overlapping account
addresses and transaction nonces, encrypted deposits to both, invalid routing,
failed withdrawals, operator/key rotation, and atomic internal transfers.

# Observability

Group-mode interfaces MUST emit the following public semantic events. Their
final Solidity signatures and indexing are part of the ABI review gate:

| Event | Required fields | Operational question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GroupCreated | Group ID, portal, admin, operator configuration version | Which public group was provisioned? |
| GroupBatchSubmitted | Group ID, batch number, previous/next commitments, Tempo checkpoint, processed deposit number, combined withdrawal queue index/root | Did shared settlement advance continuously? |
| GroupWithdrawalProcessed | Group ID, return reference, token, delivered amount, public recipient | Was an external obligation paid? |
| GroupReturnQueued | Group ID, return reference, token, refundable amount, deposit number | Which failed withdrawal awaits private credit? |

Existing public deposit, terminal refund, token, key, operator, and pause events
remain group-scoped and MUST NOT add member identifiers. No public event is
emitted for member creation or an internal transfer. Private authenticated
telemetry MUST report per-member progress, routing failures, pending returns,
internal-transfer outcomes, and accounting discrepancies. Access to those logs
MUST follow member data-access rules. Public metrics MUST NOT label members or
publish their balances or traffic. Alerts distinguish a member execution error
from the resulting group-wide settlement stall.

# Threat Model

- **Users and depositors:** May submit malformed routing, replay ciphertext,
  choose colliding references, double-spend, or target another member. Encryption
  authentication, signatures, nonces, and group conservation must reject these.
- **Operator and sequencers:** Know private routing and state and control
  inclusion and availability. They MUST NOT be able to authorize unsupported
  credits, redirect refunds, or debit other users merely by signing a batch.
  A compromised privacy operator can disclose member information.
- **Portal admin:** Controls group-wide configuration and authorizes member
  creation. That authority does not grant arbitrary member balance mutations.
  Existing administrative recovery powers, if retained, MUST be documented as
  an explicit exception to proof-enforced safety before activation.
- **Prover/verifier:** Must enforce every member transition and global routing
  invariant. A soundness failure can threaten all funds in that group; separate
  logical state does not contain that loss. Other operator groups retain
  separate escrow.
- **Public observers and callback recipients:** See group transactions, public
  token movements, opaque references, and callback data. Explicit source-zone
  disclosure is prohibited, but correlation and voluntary disclosure remain.
- **Token issuers:** Retain token-policy, pause, mint, and burn authority. This
  TIP does not remove those powers or imply unconditional redeemability.

# Invariants

The implementation and proof-system test suite MUST cover:

1. Every accepted batch opens its predecessor commitment, includes every member,
   and advances one common authenticated deposit prefix without omission.
2. A valid encrypted deposit credits only its authenticated destination; all
   other members advance without effects. Invalid routing creates one refund.
3. A withdrawal is authorized by one member debit and appears once in the
   combined queue. No queue field or proof input labels that member.
4. A return reference has one immutable private destination, cannot be reused,
   and produces at most one credit of the authenticated token and amount.
5. Successful delivery and a return credit are mutually exclusive. Failed
   deposit refunds and failed withdrawal returns terminate without a loop.
6. Per-token accounting includes every outstanding obligation exactly once;
   internal transfers preserve aggregate backing and cannot spend source escrow
   twice. Equal account addresses in different members remain distinct accounts.
7. Internal-transfer rejection restores source funds; success debits and credits
   atomically. Replays, batch failure, reorganization, and recovery cannot leave
   a one-sided accepted transfer or an unresolved internal-transfer reservation.
8. Member creation, operator changes, stale signatures, disabled tokens, policy
   rejection, malformed ciphertext, empty member blocks, queue saturation, and
   failed callbacks preserve the above properties.
9. Changing any signed public settlement input invalidates the certificate.
   Independent mutation of a private member transition invalidates the proof.
10. Replicated recovery produces the same commitment, routing, transfer order,
    and pending-return destinations as uninterrupted execution.
11. Public calldata, logs, storage, RPC responses, and proof inputs contain no
    explicit private member identifiers or per-member balances. This is a
    structural privacy test, not a claim to defeat statistical correlation.

Success requires conformance tests for these cases, engineering and security
review of the group verifier, and bounded performance measurements at the
selected limits before this TIP can be scheduled.
