# TIP-1149: Logical Zones with Shared Settlement

## Abstract

Allow multiple logical zones run by the same operator to share one portal,
deposit queue, and withdrawal queue. Each logical zone is an ordinary factory
zone with its own zone ID that joins an existing portal instead of receiving its
own. A shared batch verifies blocks from every member zone while keeping deposit
routing, withdrawal origins, and per-zone balances private. Logical zones retain
separate execution and account access. Token transfers between members settle
atomically inside a shared batch without an individual public transaction or
message.

## Motivation

A separate portal for each zone exposes that zone's aggregate balances and the
association between its users' public deposits and withdrawals and the zone.
Encrypting the recipient alone does not hide which enterprise a portal serves.
Enterprises should be able to retain separate execution environments without
publishing their individual escrow balances or identifying every entry and exit.

The intended unit of shared custody is one operator group. Pooling unrelated
operators would expand a proof-system failure's impact across operator trust
boundaries. This TIP does not provide that form of pooling.

Sharing a portal address without changing settlement is insufficient: public
per-zone withdrawal roots, queue cursors, or callback arguments would preserve
the association. The group therefore has one public settlement transition and
keeps its member transitions private.

## Assumptions

- All members use the same operator, sequencer set and threshold, proving
  configuration, deposit encryption keys, and finalized Tempo checkpoint. The
  operator already has access to every member's data. This TIP provides privacy
  against public observers, not against that operator.
- The verifier authenticates the complete group transition. A sequencer quorum
  alone MUST NOT authorize arbitrary state or withdrawals. A prototype verifier
  that accepts unconditionally is insufficient to enable this feature.
- The proof backend keeps member state and execution private. Public proof
  inputs MUST be limited to group-level commitments and public queue data;
  individual execution traces or per-zone proof outputs MUST NOT be published.
- Replication and recovery include all committed private group data. Losing
  decryption keys or private state can halt settlement; this TIP does not solve
  data availability or introduce a permissionless escape hatch.
- Shared custody and settlement deliberately couple member safety and liveness.
  A group-wide proof failure or pause can affect all members. Separate logical
  execution does not restore the isolation of separate portals.

# Specification

## 1. Group identity and state

A **logical zone** is an ordinary [TIP-1091](./tip-1091.md) zone, with its own
factory-assigned `zoneId` and the zone chain ID derived from it, whose factory
record points to an existing group portal instead of a portal of its own. A
**group** is the set of zones sharing one portal, and the portal address
identifies it. Its zones are called members below.

Zone IDs, the membership list, and each member's factory record are public.
This TIP keeps private which member a deposit, withdrawal, or return belongs
to, and each member's state and balances.

Group portals use a new, explicitly versioned settlement and withdrawal ABI.
Legacy portals retain their existing ABI and behavior. A group portal MUST NOT
accept legacy single-zone settlements or withdrawals.

Public group state contains:

- the active operator configuration and its monotonically increasing version;
- the group proof verifier and configuration hash;
- the group encryption-key history;
- enabled tokens, common portal policies, fees, and pause state;
- the membership list, its hash chain, and pending member approvals;
- the shared deposit queue, processed cursor, and withdrawal queue;
- a `uint64 batchNumber` and one `bytes32 groupStateCommitment`.

Private group state contains each member's canonical block hash, state root,
and common deposit cursor; a group return registry and return-reference key;
and a commitment salt. Member state includes its own balances, transaction
nonces, access rules, and transfer policies. Private state MUST cover every
member in the public membership list, including members unchanged by ordinary
user transactions.

The group commitment MUST be binding and hiding. In particular, it MUST NOT be
an unsalted hash of a publicly enumerable member configuration or balance vector.
Its opening is authenticated private input to the group verifier. Commitment
salts MUST remain private to the operator and be refreshed for each accepted
batch. The exact commitment tree and encoding are an implementation-readiness
item below.

No public group event, return value, storage slot, proof input, or settlement
certificate may contain an individual member's state root, block height,
balance, deposit cursor, or withdrawal root, or attribute a deposit,
withdrawal, or return to a member.

## 2. Creation and administration

The first zone of a group is created through the existing factory creation
path with group mode selected, which etches the portal at that zone's TIP-1091
address as today. Group mode is fixed at creation and publicly distinguishable
from legacy mode. This TIP does not silently convert an existing legacy portal.

Each further member joins without a new portal, in two steps:

1. The portal admin, typically the group's management multisig, calls
   `approveLogicalZone(approvalHash)` on the group portal, where:

   ```text
   approvalHash = keccak256(abi.encode(APPROVAL_DOMAIN, chainId, portal, admin,
                                       operatorConfigVersion, rpcUrl,
                                       genesisCommitment))
   ```

   The portal records the approval and emits `LogicalZoneApproved`.
2. An authorized factory caller calls
   `createLogicalZone(portal, rpcUrl, genesisCommitment)`. The factory
   recomputes the approval hash with the portal's current admin and operator
   configuration version, requires a matching unconsumed approval, and consumes
   it. It then assigns the next zone ID, records the zone with `portal` set to
   the group portal, appends the zone ID to the portal's membership hash chain,
   and emits `ZoneCreated`. It does not etch a portal.

The admin approves with a transaction instead of a signature passed to the
factory because the factory is a precompile that cannot make external EVM calls.
It therefore cannot verify an EIP-1271 signature from a contract admin, but it
can read portal storage. A transaction from the admin works for multisig,
contract, and EOA admins alike. An approval stops matching if the admin or the
operator configuration changes, and exactly one creation consumes it. The
existing factory caller restriction still applies, so during the permissioned
launch both the factory owner and the portal admin must act.

`genesisCommitment` is a hiding commitment to the member's genesis state,
including its initial member policy configuration; the first batch that
includes the member verifies that its initial state opens it. The member's
chain ID is derived from its zone ID as for any zone. Its factory record's
admin, sequencer, threshold, verifier, and access-mode fields are copied from
the portal at creation, and the portal's current configuration is authoritative
for every member.

New members start with zero token supply and the group's current processed
deposit cursor. They cannot claim deposits processed before they joined. A
batch applies every membership addition up to its Tempo checkpoint at the batch
boundary, before routing that batch's new deposits. Creation and its first
deposits MAY settle in the same batch in that order.

Member removal, migration between groups, and independent member operator or
proof configurations are out of scope. Existing operator-set replacement and
key rotation apply to the whole group and invalidate certificates under the
previous configuration.

## 3. Shared batches

A shared batch contains a nonempty ordered sequence of blocks for every member
active during that batch. Members without user transactions execute system-only
blocks to advance their common deposit cursor. All members finish at the same
processed deposit number and authenticated Tempo checkpoint. A member cannot
be omitted to bypass deposit handling, private policies, or accounting checks.

The verifier MUST establish:

1. The previous private state opens the portal's previous group commitment.
2. Every member in the public membership list at the batch's checkpoint
   follows its own valid chain transition, including signature, nonce, token,
   and access-policy rules, from its previously committed head, or from its
   genesis commitment if it joins in this batch.
3. Every member scans exactly the same newly consumed deposit prefix in order.
4. Each deposit or return has exactly one terminal group-level outcome.
5. The combined withdrawal queue contains exactly the authorized withdrawals
   and deposit refunds produced by these transitions, each once.
6. Internal transfers conserve value and finish within the batch.
7. The next commitment covers the complete next private group state.

Settlement replaces the previous commitment and appends one combined withdrawal
queue root atomically. Public inputs bind the Tempo chain ID, portal, ABI
version, operator configuration version, batch number, previous and next
commitments, finalized Tempo checkpoint and anchor, deposit prefix hashes and
cursors, token-enablement and membership progress, combined withdrawal root, and
verifier configuration hash. Threshold signatures MUST bind this same complete
statement using a separate group-settlement domain. Replays, stale
configurations, discontinuous cursors, and substitution of any field MUST fail
without changing portal state.

Existing anchor validation, queue-capacity bounds, and sequencing requirements
continue to apply at group scope. A group-wide token enablement is acknowledged
only when every member has processed it. Per-zone transitions remain private
witnesses even if the implementation internally aggregates multiple proofs.

## 4. Encrypted deposit discrimination

User deposits share one portal queue and one group encryption-key history.
Keep the existing ECIES/ECDH and authenticated-encryption construction, but use
a distinct group-deposit domain and this fixed 64-byte plaintext:

```text
version:        uint8       = 1
zoneId:         uint32      (big-endian)
recipient:      address     (20 bytes)
memo:           bytes32
reserved:       bytes7      (all zero)
```

The recipient and zone ID are authenticated ciphertext. Public key indices MUST
select group keys, never keys specific to one member. Domain separation MUST
bind the Tempo chain, portal, format version, public sender, token, amount, and
Tempo refund recipient in addition to the existing key and ephemeral-key inputs.
Existing replay protection for routed deposits MUST be preserved with
group-scoped source-withdrawal identity.

Each member advances its cursor for every entry. A valid deposit for another
member is a no-op for that member, not a rejection or refund. Only the named
member attempts the credit and applies its admission and token policies.

Authentication failure, unknown version, nonzero reserved bytes, a zone ID that
is not a member of this group, or destination rejection creates exactly one
refund obligation to the public Tempo refund recipient. A designated group
routing step handles invalid or unknown destinations before member execution so
no member independently refunds the same entry. Every queue entry advances
despite rejection.

Public sender, token, amount, and Tempo refund recipient remain visible. No
public receipt or rejection reason identifies the selected zone.

## 5. Withdrawals and private bounce-back routing

Members produce withdrawals into one combined queue, ordered as specified
below. Public encoding exposes only the flattened queue and MUST NOT contain
member boundaries or per-member counts. Public withdrawal fields retain the
token, amount, recipient, callback gas and data, and replace the zone-local
`uint64 fallbackNonce` with a `bytes32 returnRef`. As with `fallbackNonce`,
zero is reserved: user withdrawals MUST carry a nonzero `returnRef`, and
`returnRef == 0` marks a deposit refund, which the portal processes through the
terminal refund path.

The source member assigns the reference when it accepts the withdrawal, as the
zone outbox assigns `fallbackNonce` today. Each member keeps a monotonically
increasing withdrawal counter in its own state, starting at 1, and computes:

```text
returnRef = keccak256(RETURN_REF_DOMAIN || returnRefKey || zoneId || counter)
```

`zoneId` is the big-endian `uint32` and `counter` a big-endian `uint64`.
The domain constant is part of the hash-domain readiness item below.

The counter increment, reference allocation, and debit happen in the same
transaction, and the verifier recomputes the reference from committed state.
References are therefore unique across members by construction, without a
group-wide uniqueness check or a history of retired references. The member's
withdrawal receipt returns the reference so its requester can track the
withdrawal on Tempo.

`returnRefKey` is a 32-byte secret in private group state. It is what hides the
source member: zone IDs are public and counters are sequential, so an unkeyed
hash of the ID and counter could be enumerated to attribute every withdrawal to
its member. The key is supplied to member execution as a private system input
and MUST NOT appear in member state, blocks, receipts, or RPC responses. It is
long-lived; if it is replaced, counters continue, so references remain unique.

Each batch's combined queue is the batch's deposit refunds, in the order of the
deposits they refund, followed by its user withdrawals in a keyed random merge
that preserves each member's own order. Ordering by member would place each
member's withdrawals in one contiguous run, so attributing one withdrawal would
attribute its neighbours. The merge works as follows:

1. Each member orders its withdrawals for the batch by block, transaction, and
   record. For a member with `n` withdrawals, it computes
   `keccak256(ORDER_DOMAIN || returnRefKey || batchNumber || zoneId || j)`
   for `j = 1..n`, with `batchNumber` and `j` as big-endian `uint64`, sorts
   those values ascending, and assigns them to its withdrawals in order.
2. The combined queue lists all members' user withdrawals in ascending order
   of their assigned values, breaking ties by `returnRef`.

Sorting independent pseudorandom values and assigning them in order yields a
uniformly random interleaving of the members' sequences, so a withdrawal's
neighbours are no more likely to share its member than the member's share of
the batch implies. Each member computes its values without knowledge of other
members, and the verifier recomputes them from committed state and checks the
public order. The assigned values are private and never published. Deposit
refunds already identify their public deposit, so placing them first by deposit
order reveals nothing and keeps a destination-rejection refund out of its
member's withdrawals.

Withdrawals from one member reach Tempo in that member's order, as they do for
a single zone today. The relative order of withdrawals from different members
within a batch is unspecified.

The private registry binds each reference to its source member and private
fallback recipient, as the zone outbox's fallback-recipient mapping does today.
It does not store the token or amount: a return carries both from the consumed
withdrawal, which the portal authenticates. Withdrawal fees follow the group fee
schedule, cannot expose a member-specific fee recipient, and are not refunded on
return.

For authenticated callbacks, sender tags and optional encrypted sender data
MUST use group-scoped domains and MUST NOT encode the source member. The shared
messenger authenticates the group portal and passes the reserved zone ID `0` in
place of the source zone ID; receivers identify the group by `sourcePortal`.
Contracts requiring a nonzero source zone ID or a public source-member identity
are incompatible with this private callback mode; no implicit disclosure mode
is introduced by this TIP.

A successful delivery requires no group-side action, and the group does not
need to learn of it. As with fallback-recipient entries today, the entry for a
successful withdrawal stays in the registry; no rule depends on removing it. A
failed delivery leaves funds in escrow and appends a group return entry
containing `(returnRef, token, refundableAmount)` to the shared deposit queue,
where `refundableAmount` is the withdrawal's `amount`. The portal derives these
fields from the consumed withdrawal, not from unauthenticated sequencer input.
The entry is public but contains no zone ID or fallback recipient.

Every member scans the return entry. The committed registry privately selects
exactly one source member and recipient. That member credits the entry's token
and amount to the recipient once, and the entry is deleted from the registry.
If the destination mint is blocked, park the credit in that member's private
refund registry; never bounce it again. Each reference is derived once and its
entry is deleted only by its own return, so a correct implementation never sees
a return for an unknown reference. If one appears, the batch is invalid.

Delivery effects and failure detection retain the existing revert-isolated
withdrawal behavior. Successful callbacks cannot both keep an outgoing transfer
and produce its return. Portal queue entries are consumed once, and existing
capacity reservations MUST cover the maximum number of possible returns.
A retry cannot allocate a second refund. Failed deposit refunds retain the
existing terminal Tempo refund registry, always carry `returnRef == 0`, and
MUST NOT create a user return reference or a second bounce-back cycle.

Private routing information MUST be part of committed, replicated state, not
only a sequencer-local lookup. Replacement sequencers must recover pending
returns from that state and finalized Tempo history.

## 6. Direct transfers between logical zones

A member exposes a private token-transfer operation whose signed request binds
source chain, group portal, destination zone ID, token, amount, recipient, and
the sender's ordinary transaction nonce. Only members of the same group are
valid destinations. The source enforces the sender's ordinary signature,
balance, allowance where applicable, and policy rules. The destination enforces
its admission, token, and recipient policies.

Transfers use a deterministic two-phase batch schedule:

1. After processing the shared deposit prefix, members execute ordinary blocks.
   A direct-transfer request reserves its amount against the source balance
   and emits a private transfer record. Reservation is a system balance lock,
   not a transfer to another account; it cannot be spent or withdrawn.
2. After all ordinary blocks, the group processes records in ascending source
   zone ID, source block number, transaction index, and request index within
   the transaction. Source and destination system blocks settle each record
   against the policies in effect after ordinary execution. On acceptance,
   burn the reserved source amount and mint exactly that amount in the
   destination. On rejection, clear the reservation without a token transfer;
   a policy change therefore cannot prevent releasing the lock. No destination
   user code is called.
3. All records finish before the next group commitment is accepted. Credits are
   spendable by ordinary transactions starting in the next shared batch.

Source and destination success are one atomic group transition. No internal
transfer changes portal escrow, appends to either public queue, invokes a public
callback, or causes its own Tempo event. The ordinary group commitment changes.
A rejected request does not invalidate unrelated transfers; an invalid proof or
missing system outcome invalidates the entire batch. Ordinary execution fees
follow the member's private rules; no public bridge fee is charged.

There are no pending transfers across accepted group batches, destination
contract calls, cross-group transfers, or general cross-zone messages. If a
batch fails or is reorganized, all its source reservations and destination
credits revert together. Recovery re-executes the same ordered records.

## 7. Accounting, policies, and privacy

The verifier enforces conservation separately for each token and each member.
Member supply changes only through an authenticated deposit, its own authorized
withdrawal, a matched internal transfer, or an authenticated return. Sharing
custody never permits an unauthorized debit from another member.

Group escrow accounting also includes public deposits not yet credited,
committed withdrawals not yet paid, returns not yet credited, parked refunds,
and fee liabilities. A transition MUST NOT count an amount in two liability
categories. Paying or refunding an external obligation removes exactly its
corresponding liability; an internal transfer changes neither total group
liabilities nor portal escrow. Token-issuer burn or freeze powers retain their
existing trust assumptions and may break solvency or liveness independently.

Portal-level policies remain common and public. Member-specific policies are
checked privately in the group transition. A portal-level policy that depends on
which member a deposit or withdrawal belongs to cannot be enforced without
weakening this TIP's privacy goal. RPC access remains scoped to each member and
account; shared proving authority does not grant ordinary users access to other
members' state or queue routing. Group decryption witnesses, routing-system
inputs, return-registry openings, the return-reference key, and commitment salts
MUST NOT be exposed through member block or trace RPCs.

The privacy guarantee removes explicit public member attribution. It does not
guarantee statistical unlinkability: amounts, token choice, counterparties,
callback data, timing, operator disclosures, and low group activity can identify
a member. Internal transfers MUST NOT force separate public submissions, and
adding a member MUST NOT require a settlement of its own. Operators SHOULD use a
common settlement cadence; padding and cover traffic are not required by this
TIP.

## 8. Compatibility and activation

Activation requires a coordinated protocol upgrade to the factory, portal,
messenger, verifier, sequencer, prover, and clients. Group creation uses an
explicit new mode and group settlement version; legacy creation remains
available. Existing portals and existing deposits, withdrawals, and certificates
continue under their original rules. No existing escrow or live queue is moved
by this TIP. Migrating a legacy zone into a group requires a separate proposal.

Shared zones change two TIP-1091 assumptions. A member's portal is not at
`ZONE_PORTAL_PREFIX || zoneId`; that derivation holds only for the zone that
created the portal. Clients, nodes, and indexers MUST read a zone's portal from
`zones(zoneId).portal`, and a zone node MUST validate its chain ID against its
own zone ID rather than one read from the portal. `isZonePortal` is unchanged.
The shared messenger MUST accept zone ID `0` from a group-mode portal and keeps
its existing check that any nonzero zone ID belongs to the calling portal.

Each member's factory record supplies its RPC endpoint, and the group portal
supplies the common encryption keys. Deposit routing needs only the public zone
ID.

## 9. Items required before implementation readiness

This is a protocol design draft, not an activation-ready ABI. Before advancing
to implementation readiness, specify and review:

- exact group creation, `approveLogicalZone`, `createLogicalZone`, group
  settlement, withdrawal, and messenger interfaces, error selectors, and
  storage layouts;
- the hiding commitment tree, canonical encodings, hash domains, encryption
  transcript, proof public-input encoding, and reference test vectors;
- return-registry growth from successful withdrawals, and any pruning rule,
  which MUST NOT delete an entry whose return may still be queued;
- measured limits for members, blocks, proof work, deposits, withdrawals, and
  internal transfer records, including worst-case queue scanning;
- compatibility with planned forced exits, recovery, and public counterparties;
- the implementation owner, reviewed proof backend, and target protocol version.

These are review gates, not permission to omit the specified routing,
conservation, privacy, or atomicity requirements.

# Tooling

Wallets and SDKs MUST resolve a zone's portal through its factory record and
support versioned group deposit encryption, return references from member
withdrawal receipts, group callback encoding, and internal transfer requests.
They MUST report internal transfers as pending until the whole shared batch
settles and distinguish rejection from settlement failure. Public receipt and
explorer interfaces MUST show group-level progress without inventing per-member
balances or attribution.

Provisioning MUST add members through a portal-admin approval followed by
factory creation. Sequencers, replicas, and provers MUST use the same
membership, queue cursor, transfer and withdrawal ordering, and commitment
format. Backup and restore MUST include the member heads, salts, encryption-key
history, return registry, return-reference key, and all member states at a
consistent group checkpoint. Recovery tooling MUST verify that the restored
state opens the accepted portal commitment before producing blocks.

Conformance fixtures MUST include at least two members with overlapping account
addresses and transaction nonces, encrypted deposits to both, invalid routing,
failed withdrawals, multiple withdrawals from each member in one batch,
operator/key rotation, and atomic internal transfers.

# Observability

Group-mode interfaces MUST emit the following public semantic events. Their
final Solidity signatures and indexing are part of the ABI review gate:

| Event | Required fields | Operational question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ZoneCreated (existing) | Zone ID, portal, admin, sequencers, threshold, RPC URL | Which zones exist, and which portal serves each? |
| LogicalZoneApproved | Portal, admin, approval hash | Did the portal admin approve a new member? |
| GroupBatchSubmitted | Portal, batch number, previous/next commitments, Tempo checkpoint, processed deposit number, combined withdrawal queue index/root | Did shared settlement advance continuously? |
| GroupWithdrawalProcessed | Portal, nonzero return reference, token, delivered amount, public recipient | Was a user withdrawal paid? |
| GroupReturnQueued | Portal, return reference, token, refundable amount, deposit number | Which failed withdrawal awaits private credit? |

Existing public deposit, terminal refund, token, key, operator, and pause events
remain group-scoped and MUST NOT add member identifiers. Deposit refunds
(`returnRef == 0`) emit the existing terminal refund events, not
GroupWithdrawalProcessed. No public event is emitted for an internal transfer.
Private authenticated telemetry MUST report per-member progress, routing
failures, pending returns, internal-transfer outcomes, and accounting
discrepancies. Access to those logs MUST follow member data-access rules. Public
metrics MUST NOT label members or publish their balances or traffic. Alerts
distinguish a member execution error from the resulting group-wide settlement
stall.

# Threat Model

- **Users and depositors:** May submit malformed routing, replay ciphertext,
  double-spend, or target another member. Encryption authentication,
  signatures, nonces, and group conservation must reject these.
- **Operator and sequencers:** Know private routing and state and control
  inclusion and availability. They MUST NOT be able to authorize unsupported
  credits, redirect refunds, or debit other users merely by signing a batch.
  A compromised privacy operator can disclose member information.
- **Portal admin:** Controls group-wide configuration and approves each new
  member on Tempo. That authority does not grant arbitrary member balance
  mutations.
  Existing administrative recovery powers, if retained, MUST be documented as
  an explicit exception to proof-enforced safety before activation.
- **Factory caller:** During the permissioned launch only the factory owner
  can create zones, and it cannot add a member to a group portal without a
  matching approval from that portal's admin.
- **Prover/verifier:** Must enforce every member transition and global routing
  invariant. A soundness failure can threaten all funds in that group; separate
  logical state does not contain that loss. Other operator groups retain
  separate escrow.
- **Public observers and callback recipients:** See group transactions, public
  token movements, opaque references, and callback data. Explicit source-zone
  disclosure is prohibited, but correlation and voluntary disclosure remain.
- **Token issuers:** Retain token-policy, pause, mint, and burn authority. This
  TIP does not remove those powers or imply unconditional redeemability.

# Invariants

The implementation and proof-system test suite MUST cover:

1. Every accepted batch opens its predecessor commitment, includes every member,
   and advances one common authenticated deposit prefix without omission.
2. A valid encrypted deposit credits only its authenticated destination; all
   other members advance without effects. Invalid routing creates one refund.
3. A withdrawal is authorized by one member debit and appears once in the
   combined queue. No queue field or proof input labels that member, and its
   position follows the keyed merge: deposit refunds first by deposit order,
   then user withdrawals in each member's own order, interleaved by their
   keyed values.
4. A return reference is derived once per member withdrawal, has one immutable
   private destination, never recurs across members, and produces at most one
   credit of the authenticated token and amount.
5. Successful delivery and a return credit are mutually exclusive. Failed
   deposit refunds and failed withdrawal returns terminate without a loop.
6. Per-token accounting includes every outstanding obligation exactly once;
   internal transfers preserve aggregate backing and cannot spend source escrow
   twice. Equal account addresses in different members remain distinct accounts.
7. Internal-transfer rejection restores source funds; success debits and credits
   atomically. Replays, batch failure, reorganization, and recovery cannot leave
   a one-sided accepted transfer or an unresolved internal-transfer reservation.
8. Member creation, operator changes, stale signatures, disabled tokens, policy
   rejection, malformed ciphertext, empty member blocks, queue saturation, and
   failed callbacks preserve the above properties.
9. Changing any signed public settlement input invalidates the certificate.
   Independent mutation of a private member transition invalidates the proof.
10. Replicated recovery produces the same commitment, routing, transfer and
    withdrawal order, and pending-return destinations as uninterrupted
    execution.
11. Public calldata, logs, storage, RPC responses, and proof inputs attribute
    no deposit, withdrawal, or return to a member and contain no per-member
    state or balances. This is a structural privacy test, not a claim to defeat
    statistical correlation.
12. A zone joins a group portal only through a matching, unconsumed approval
    from the portal's current admin under the current operator configuration.
    Settlement binds the membership hash chain, and a joining member's initial
    state opens its genesis commitment.

Success requires conformance tests for these cases, engineering and security
review of the group verifier, and bounded performance measurements at the
selected limits before this TIP can be scheduled.
